Exceptional-point sensing

Gain and loss, out of balance

Below the exceptional point (g < γ) the eigenvalues are imaginary. One mode grows while the other decays, and PT symmetry is broken.

Two modes become one

At g = γ the eigenvalues and their eigenvectors coalesce. This degeneracy is the exceptional point.

A square-root response

Just beyond it the splitting grows far faster than a linear shift. I use quantum Fisher information to test whether this becomes a real sensing advantage once noise is included.

λ± = ±√(g² − γ²)

  2. 2026

    Quantum coherence-assisted entanglement in a GaAs/AlGaAs quantum dot molecule via quantum entropy

    H. Abroo, A. Iqbal Bashir* MS PHYSB-D-26-03159

    Submitted to Physica B: Condensed Matter (Elsevier), 2026

    Can the Schmidt decomposition give a cleaner account of photon–quantum-dot entanglement than the usual steady-state master-equation treatment?

    Bipartite entanglement entropy between a quantum system, such as an atom or a quantum dot, and its emitted photon has attracted considerable research focus. Most previous studies have employed the master-equation density-matrix formalism for the steady-state solution and the reduced density matrix of the individual systems. To avoid some of the underlying assumptions of that formalism, which represent a trade-off between accuracy and simplicity, this work employs the more direct approach of the Schmidt decomposition method to investigate quantum-coherence-controlled bipartite entanglement between a GaAs/AlGaAs quantum dot molecule (QDM) and its emitted photon field.

    An optically driven three-level cascade (Ξ) QDM system serves as the solid-state quantum platform, subject to a weak probe field and a strong control field. In the dressed-state quantum-coherence picture, the control field dresses the excited levels {|1⟩, |2⟩} of the QDM, and the spontaneously emitted photonic field entangles this dressed two-level QDM subsystem with its emitted photon in a pure bipartite state. The degree of entanglement is quantified by the von Neumann entropy obtained from an explicit Schmidt decomposition.

    The entanglement entropy reaches its maximum value of one bit at control-field resonance (Δc = 0) and is an even function of Δc; the width of the resonance is set by the control Rabi frequency Ωc, and the entanglement decreases monotonically toward zero at large detuning. The dressed-state eigenvalues depend on Ωc but are independent of the probe Rabi frequency Ωp, which enters the linearized dynamics only as an inhomogeneous drive and therefore sets the magnitude of the driven coherences without shifting the dressed-resonance positions or linewidths.

    These results establish a direct operational link between field-tunable quantum coherence and quantum entropy, with implications for coherence-controlled quantum communication, quantum computing, and nanoscale thermodynamic devices.
